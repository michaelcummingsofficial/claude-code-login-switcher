All user-facing copy (the README, notifications, quick pick and input box text, status bar tooltips, command titles, setting descriptions, and the Marketplace description in `package.json`) follows one voice: calm, confident, plain, and short. Think of how Apple writes a product page: one idea per sentence, concrete words, and the confidence to stop. State the benefit and let it land. When writing or editing any copy, apply these rules.

### Short sentences, one idea each

Write the way Apple does. A sentence carries one idea and ends. Three short sentences beat one long one with clauses. The README intro is two or three sentences and under about 30 words. Section intros are one or two sentences. A notification is one or two sentences.

Before: "A powerful, flexible extension that lets developers seamlessly manage, switch between, and securely store multiple Claude Code accounts with automatic token synchronization."

After: "Switch Claude Code accounts from the status bar. Your history stays put. Your tokens stay in the Keychain."

### Concrete verbs and nouns

Say what the person does, not what the product is. "Switch accounts" beats "account switching functionality." "Log in again" beats "re-authentication required." Lists of three verbs read well; lists of five features do not.

### One emphasis, if any

A paragraph may carry one phrase in ink for emphasis, the way Apple bolds a single claim inside gray body copy. Never two. Never a whole sentence in bold.

### No jargon up front

Technical terms are fine in How it works, the settings table, and CONTRIBUTING. They do not belong in the README intro, the Marketplace description, or a notification's first sentence. Say what the term means for the reader instead: "your tokens stay in the macOS Keychain" rather than "credentials persisted as generic password items."

### No em dashes

Never use `—` (em dash) in copy. It reads as machine-written. Replace it with the punctuation a person would actually use:

- Period when the two halves are full thoughts
- Comma when one half depends on the other
- Colon when the second half explains or lists

This applies to copy only. Standalone `—` as a "no value" marker in data tables is fine, and code comments are out of scope.

### Confidence, not contempt

State a benefit calmly. Never oversell with absolutes or drama. Never name or mock the enemy, including other extensions. No stacked adjectives: one is plenty, none is often better.

### Calm buttons

Short, lowercase-feeling verbs. Two or three words where possible ("Save it first", "Verify", "Remove"). No shouting, no exclamation points.

### Clean sentences

Write parallel, complete sentences. Don't stack fragments and dashes for drama. Read it aloud; if you run out of breath, split it.
